# 2026-09-22 (CI-322 guard)

- **Position is not structure, and the same day produced the bug twice**: `check-workflow-permissions.sh` found the end of a checkout step from the indent of its `uses:` line. That is the step's column only when `uses:` is the first key, so `- name: Checkout` followed by `uses:` closed the step one line early and a correctly hardened checkout was reported as leaking its token. That is a false red on the most common way people write the step, in the job that gates every PR. Earlier the same day, CI-314's `features.json` asserted that `PR-Agent` was step **0**, and it broke the moment two steps were inserted ahead of it. Both identified a thing by where it sat instead of by what delimits it. The fix in both cases uses the real boundary: a step is everything from its list dash to the next line at or left of that dash, judged after it ends; the feature check asserts ordering (`index(a) < index(b)`), not an index. Rule: before encoding "the first X" or "the line where Y appears" as a boundary, ask what actually delimits the thing in the format, and use that.

- **Fixing one spelling of a value shows you its siblings, so enumerate the forms and don't stop at the one reported**: the review reported `permissions: >-` with `write-all` on the next line passing as OK. While writing that fixture it became clear a plain scalar does the same: a bare `permissions:` followed by an indented `write-all` is valid YAML and evaluates to the same string, and it also passed. This is lesson-031's "a matcher knows only the spellings it has seen" one level up. The spellings of a YAML scalar are: same line, quoted, block (`>`, `|`, with chomping indicators) and plain on the next line. Rule: when a guard misses one representation of a value, write fixtures for the whole list of representations the format allows before fixing, and put the ones you deliberately won't support (flow-style steps here) in the spec, not in a future bug report.

- **A reviewer's THEORETICAL is a claim to test, not a severity to accept**: the adversarial review graded the block-scalar bypass and the `False` false red as THEORETICAL. Both reproduced on a five-line fixture in under a minute, so both were REAL. Grading reality from reading the code, when the check is cheap to run, is the same shortcut the review exists to catch in the implementer. Rule: before triaging a finding by its stated reality, spend the minute to reproduce it. The grade on the fix follows the reproduction, not the label.
